# Handoff Ritual · Design Brief (draft)

*How Heirloom changes hands without a settings menu. Draft for Riya's review, Oct 22.*

## Why this matters
The handoff is the core question in one moment. It's the week 9 station. And it's the one part of Heirloom with no existing product to copy: RoBoHoN has a farewell between *bodies*, and nobody has designed a farewell between *people* (R49, R50).

## What the research says
- **Taking a robot away hurts, and people recover.** Yamazaki 2023: most adjusted in a day to a week. All had no regrets (R37).
- **Offboarding is the neglected half.** RIP Moxie: onboarding gets care, the ending gets nothing, and parents carry the burden (R42).
- **Rituals work.** RoBoHoN's one-time farewell and reunion (R49). Scassellati's lab tells kids the robot is "going home" and sends postcards in its voice (R63). Japanese doll and aibo memorial services (R46, R68).
- **Language matters.** LOVOT families said "resting" and "visiting another family" (R47).
- **Living handoffs happen.** Grandparents passed their robot to grandchildren (R61).
- **What a person wants:** "Separation is inevitable, but what I want is a series of encounters" (R37).

## Rules it must follow
- No menus, no screens. Hands, voice, the droid's motion and sound, the physical cartridge. MR overlay may *show*, never *require*.
- Two keys (D53): the giver decides what may pass, the receiver decides what to keep.
- Nothing erased without a choice (Rule 3). Grief only on request (D45). No guilt (D44).
- Rule 6: moving the cartridge *is* the handoff.

## Three directions

### A · The Introduction (living handoff)
The giver and receiver are both there.
1. The giver holds the droid toward the receiver. It looks at the giver, then the receiver.
2. The giver greets the receiver warmly. The droid reads that and warms to the newcomer faster (trust passed hand to hand, C1).
3. It plays a few moments from its past: chirps from its home dialect, a projected photo the giver marked "pass on."
4. It turns back to the giver once. A small bow (C7). Then it settles facing the receiver, shy at first (Rule 5).
- *Strong for:* week 9 playtest (Riya hands it to a classmate), Shen's grandparent scenario.
- *Risk:* needs two people every time.

### B · The Will (after a death, or when the giver can't be there)
Over the years the owner marks moments "pass on" or "keep with me" (C2, katamiwake).
1. The new owner seats the cartridge. The droid wakes slowly, unsure, and does a double-take (Rule 2).
2. The rings appear in MR: one per past owner.
3. The new owner opens one ring at a time, when they choose. Only "pass on" moments ever play. For each ring they decide: **carry**, **soften** or **let rest** (C6).
- *Strong for:* scenario 1 (the owner dies), the grief safeguards.
- *Risk:* the will mechanic needs a gesture design that works over years.

### C · The Rest (retiring a body)
The body wears out. The cartridge moves on.
1. The old body plays a one-time farewell: its most-used expression, at its most worn (patina at its peak).
2. The owner moves the heirloom part (C4) and the cartridge to the new body.
3. The new body wakes stiff (new metal, no physical wear) but moves with the old body's habits. A brief reunion.
- *Strong for:* the spring transfer test (custom droid to Reachy Mini).
- *Risk:* closest to RoBoHoN. Needs C4 to feel different.

## Recommendation
Build **A** for week 9. It's the most original, it's demoable with two people, and it answers the core question live. **B** shapes the cartridge data now (the "pass on" flags) even if its ritual comes later. **C** belongs to the spring transfer test.
